# Article series and editorial calendar

**Public series:** Executive Talent & Enterprise Performance — research and analysis by Vaughn Berger.

## Planned sequence
- **A00:** Introduction — why examine hiring from the employer's perspective?
- **A01–A08:** One research article after each corresponding accepted research topic.
- **A09:** Synthesis of the accepted discoveries and action plan.
- **A10:** The actual implementation methodology; no success claims before testing.
- **A11+:** Results, including Weeks 1–4 observations only after each observation period is complete.

This order is a dependency sequence, not a promise of daily research completion. The author intends to schedule up to one LinkedIn article per day when approved material is ready. Dates and published links are entered in the publication log after scheduling/publishing.

## Editorial structure
1. Evidence-based title and early meaningful finding
2. Abstract and 3–6 keywords
3. Brief, engaging introduction framed around a real challenge
4. Findings, interpretation, and mandatory chart
5. Implications for enterprise leadership or job seekers
6. Key takeaways, limitations, and genuine discussion question
7. Citations and source links

Target a five-minute read, approximately 600–850 words unless clarity requires otherwise. Articles are styled as accessible research essays, not as affiliated academic papers. No misleading attribution to Harvard or another institution.

## Two audience lenses
**Enterprise:** recruiting leaders, HR executives, hiring authorities; decision quality, costs, skills and operations.  
**Career:** adults across mid- and later-career stages; empathetic and practical, avoiding promises or stereotypes.

Companion career articles should begin only when accepted evidence can support useful guidance; proposed starting point is after topics 01–03. No filler articles.

## Visuals
Every article requires at least one legible, original, accurately labeled data visualization with an independently traceable data source. No invented values, misleading scales, or unlabeled forecasts.
